Undergraduate Research · Oct 2026 – Present · In progress

Multi-Robot Research with Prof. Sanjeev Bedi

Undergraduate research assistant at the University of Waterloo, giving small TETRIX robots cameras, a sense of where they are, and a way to share a space without colliding.

  • TETRIX PRIZM
  • Raspberry Pi
  • Arduino C++
  • OpenCV
  • YOLO
  • ArUco
  • Multi-robot

The question

The term started from a simple goal: two robots navigating toward their own destinations, without knowing about each other at first, should notice one another, check whether their paths conflict, and resolve it. That grew into a shared arena where one typed request has both robots working on the same task while keeping out of each other's way.

What I've built so far

  • Drive control: Arduino C++ on the TETRIX PRIZM controller for the motors, encoders, line and ultrasonic sensors, with a watchdog that brakes if the Raspberry Pi goes quiet.
  • Vision: benchmarked detection on the Pi against offloading frames to a computer over Wi-Fi and Ethernet, first with OpenCV colour detection and then YOLO26 nano. The robots now stream several webcams to YOLO on a computer and get detections back.
  • Goals with checks: a robot server on each Pi that accepts goals such as drive, turn, find and approach, checks each against a safety policy before moving, and reports what happened.
  • Avoidance and position: camera-only avoidance using ArUco markers on each robot, and localization from markers at the arena's corners.

Where it stands

The first run on the real robots was on 15 November 2026: one complete fetch-and-deliver task finished in 57 s, with the second robot moved out of the way first. Drive distance calibration, camera focal length and one robot's link to the vision computer are still being fixed, and the camera-only avoidance rule has so far been tested in simulation only.
