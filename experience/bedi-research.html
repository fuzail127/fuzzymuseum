Undergraduate Research · Oct 2026 – Present · Early stage

Multi-Robot Coordination Research

Undergraduate research assistant with Prof. Sanjeev Bedi at the University of Waterloo, exploring how independently navigating robots can coordinate when they encounter one another.

  • TETRIX Robots
  • Multi-Robot Systems
  • Visual Detection
  • Camera Multiplexing
  • Path Coordination

The research goal

The term's goal is for two TETRIX robots to start at separate points on a grid and navigate toward their own selected destinations, initially without knowing that the other robot is operating. Once they come into view of one another, they should stop, communicate, and determine whether their intended paths conflict.

Planned sensing

The proposed setup uses 4–6 cameras per robot to work toward 360-degree visual coverage. A multiplexer will route multiple camera inputs through a single output. The camera arrangement and sensing behavior are part of the research scope; coverage and integration results are still to be established.

Target encounter behavior

The intended sequence is:

  1. Navigate independently: each robot follows a route toward its own destination.
  2. Detect and stop: visual detection of the other robot triggers a pause.
  3. Communicate: the robots exchange the information needed to check their intended paths for conflicts.
  4. Coordinate: resolve a potential conflict before continuing toward their destinations.

Current stage

I joined the research in October 2026. Independent navigation, visual encounter detection, and path coordination are the term's research objectives. This page will document hardware integration and experimental results as the work progresses.
