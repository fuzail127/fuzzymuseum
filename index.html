01 About

I'm a Mechatronics Engineering student at the University of Waterloo who likes projects that need a soldering iron, a 3D printer and a debugger in the same afternoon. I've configured embedded firmware on STM32 and ESP32, automated quantum-chemistry pipelines in Python, and helped run FEA/CFD and microcontroller workshops for engineering students.

In my last co-op I designed collaborative- and fleet-robotics challenges with a Dobot arm, OpenCV and a floor full of TETRIX robots, and built the backend for a campus environmental sensor platform. I'm now an undergraduate research assistant with Prof. Sanjeev Bedi, building a two-robot arena where TETRIX robots with Raspberry Pi cameras find objects, localize from markers and stay out of each other's way. With the UW Robotics Team, I've established micro-ROS messaging over USART3 and am working on the rover's Ethernet communication interface.

Two-Robot Arena

In progress

Research with Prof. Sanjeev Bedi · first task on the robots in 57 s · 15 Nov 2026

Two TETRIX robots, each with a Raspberry Pi and webcams, share a marked arena. One typed request, like bring the bottle to the near-left corner, becomes a plan: find the object with YOLO, send the closer robot, move the other out of the way. Each robot works out its position from the corner markers, and the safety checks run on the robot, not over Wi-Fi.

Stewart Platform Ball Balancer

MTE 380 · Nov 2026

91 waypoints in 171 s · 27 mm mean overshoot, simulation predicted 27 mm

A three-servo Stewart platform tilts a 300 mm plate while a camera tracks a ping-pong ball. I measured the plant from our own logs, found the balancing loop had too much gain for its 0.2 s of dead time and retuned it, then wrote a waypoint planner. In simulation the planner removed overshoot; on the plate, the ball rarely stopped long enough for it to run, and the page says why.

04 Case studies

Simulation case studies written at the IDEAS Clinic. Each one pairs an analytical or experimental result with a simulation, and treats the disagreement between them as the thing worth writing about.

3D-Printed Compact Heat Exchanger

Published by Ansys

A cross-flow heat exchanger printed in copper-filled PLA, measured on the bench, then rebuilt as a conjugate heat transfer simulation in Ansys Discovery. I characterized the filament's unpublished thermal properties with a thermal camera and a heated print bed, and traced the experiment–simulation gap to print porosity, backpressure and where the thermocouple was sitting.

Involute Spur Gear Root and Contact Stresses

Under review by Ansys

AGMA's ANSI/AGMA 2001-D04 method against FEA on a pair of meshing spur gears. Root stress agreed to 3.11%; contact stress was 76.2% off and never converged under mesh refinement, which was itself the clue. Rebuilding the contact definition in Ansys Mechanical brought it to 5.13%.
